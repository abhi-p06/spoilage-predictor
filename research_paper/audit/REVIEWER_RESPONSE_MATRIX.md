## 1. Historical Context and Scientific Evolution

### A. Prior Submission Provenance
The original AgriRoute manuscript (Paper ID: 1345) was a **previously submitted, unpublished manuscript** submitted to the 5th International Conference on Machine Learning and Data Engineering (ICMLDE 5.0). It received a **revision-and-resubmission** decision from the program committee, accompanied by detailed critiques from three independent peer reviewers. **As confirmed by the corresponding author, the paper was never published.** Therefore, it must not be cited as a published document, but treated as a previously submitted, unpublished manuscript. The current V4.1 manuscript develops from this previously submitted version under a major scientific reframing.

### B. The Paradigm Shift from System Integration to Empirical Science
The initial submission presented an applied multimodal software system combining:
- A MobileNetV2 produce classifier trained on an internal Kaggle split (reporting 97.22% validation accuracy).
- A BiLSTM temporal sensor-risk model trained on synthetic 24-hour sequences (reporting an unrealistic $\text{AUC} = 1.000$).
- An ad-hoc linear route-risk heuristic ($G = \text{clip}(\alpha \tau D - \beta c, 0, 100)$).
- An AGMARKNET wholesale price retrieval module.
- An XGBoost fusion layer (reporting $>95\%$ held-out classifier accuracy).
- A farmer-facing dashboard demonstrated on a single illustrative Guntur-to-Rajkot banana transit run ($N = 1$).

The reviewers correctly identified fundamental vulnerabilities: lack of scientific novelty beyond software plumbing (Reviewer 1, Comment 2; Reviewer 3, Comment 1), unrealistic synthetic metrics (Reviewer 1, Comment 3), absence of external dataset validation (Reviewer 2, Comment 3), ungrounded fusion weights (Reviewer 2, Comment 1), overreliance on a single mock shipment that was insufficient to establish generalization or field impact (Reviewer 3, Comment 2), and claiming field impact without field data (Reviewer 1, Comment 5; Reviewer 3, Comment 4).

In response, the research team did not attempt fragile patches or superficial narrative adjustments. Instead, the team executed a **complete scientific restructuring**:
1. **Decoupling Unvalidated Components:** Removed the synthetic-data BiLSTM, linear route heuristic, and unbenchmarked price forecasting from the revised manuscript and excluded them from the current empirical claims. The earlier synthetic BiLSTM results are not treated as valid evidence of physical IoT performance, and the old synthetic-data results are retained only as historical development context.
2. **Empirical Computer Vision Reframing:** Re-scoped the contribution as an empirical out-of-distribution (OOD) detection benchmark evaluating a frozen MobileNetV2 classifier across an audited $13,465$-image evaluated record census from three independent repositories (Kaggle/Primary: 227; FruitVision: 10,045; Sultana: 3,193).
3. **Rigorous Methodological Foundation:** Replaced ad-hoc fusion with five established post-hoc OOD scoring algorithms (MSP, Energy, ODIN, Mahalanobis distance, OpenMax) and an operational Energy–MSP Dual-Gate rejection cascade calibrated on the primary validation partition using empirical order statistics and held fixed during external cohort evaluation ($\tau_{95}$ operating thresholds: $\tau_{\text{Energy}} = 0.918451$, $\tau_{\text{MSP}} = 0.582610$).
4. **Source-Stratified Experimental Design:** Structured the evaluation into source-matched cohorts to measure performance differences across covariate domain shifts, unseen-species semantic shifts, and post-harvest treatment-labelled condition shifts. Source matching reduces cross-repository confounding while potential within-repository capture variation remains.
5. **Statistical and Uncertainty Rigor:** Replaced isolated point estimates with 1,000-iteration non-parametric bootstrap 95% confidence intervals and perceptual-hash (pHash) near-duplicate sensitivity auditing.

## 3. Discredited Historical Results Excluded from Current Claims

To ensure scientific integrity, the following four historical results from the 2025 unpublished manuscript are permanently excluded from current empirical claims and must **never** be cited as current evidence or conflated with V4.1 metrics:

1. **MobileNetV2 Validation Accuracy = 97.22%:**  
   *Reason for Exclusion:* Evaluated on an internal random Kaggle split. In V4.1, the frozen model achieves $96.92\%$ [$94.71\%$, $98.68\%$] on Primary Test Partition A ($N=227$), but exhibits an external known-class performance difference of $28.56\text{ pp}$, dropping to $68.36\%$ [$67.20\%$, $69.40\%$] on pooled external known-class image records (Partition B, $N=5,350$).
2. **BiLSTM Validation AUC = 1.000:**  
   *Reason for Exclusion:* Artifact of evaluating on synthetic sequences generated from deterministic non-overlapping mathematical formulas. The earlier synthetic BiLSTM results are not treated as valid evidence of physical IoT performance.
3. **XGBoost Classifier Accuracy > 95%:**  
   *Reason for Exclusion:* Evaluated on a hybrid feature space containing synthetic sensor values.
4. **Guntur-to-Rajkot Single Shipment Demonstration ($N = 1$):**  
   *Reason for Exclusion:* An illustrative software execution run (distance $1580.3\text{ km}$, visual score $65.3$, route risk $31.5$, fused risk $34.7$). Not an experimental benchmark; insufficient to establish generalization or field impact.

## 4. Re-Scoped Logistics-Platform Comparison Justification

Reviewer 3 requested a comparative table benchmarking AgriRoute against commercial agricultural logistics and supply chain platforms (e.g., AgNext, Intello Labs, CropIn, Ecozen, Stellapps).

### Why the Original Request is Addressed Through Scope Change:
In the 2025 submission, AgriRoute was presented as an end-to-end logistics and dispatch advisory system, making a commercial feature comparison seem relevant. In V4.1, however, the research scope has been **fundamentally reframed as an empirical computer vision study** investigating out-of-distribution detection, domain transfer, and rejection behavior on a frozen classifier. Commercial logistics platforms are commercial products that generally do not disclose model architectures, provide pre-trained checkpoints, or release standardized test partitions for reproducible academic benchmarking. Comparing an academic open-source vision benchmark against commercial feature lists would violate scientific reproducibility standards.

### Scholarly Replacement Matrix:
Instead of a commercial platform matrix, Section 2 of the revised manuscript will include a **Scholarly Literature Comparison Table** synthesizing reported characteristics of recent peer-reviewed agricultural vision and open-set recognition studies:
