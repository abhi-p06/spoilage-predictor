To diagnose the drivers of this external degradation, Table 10 breaks down classification performance across individual classes and species. Inspection reveals that domain degradation is highly asymmetric:
1. **Catastrophic Fresh Apple Collapse:** Fresh Apple accuracy collapses to 32.12% (310/965 correct). The classifier systematically misidentifies 40.73% of fresh apples as Rotten Apple (393 images) and 26.42% as Rotten Orange (255 images). Across the entire Apple species cohort ($N = 1,795$), overall accuracy is only 34.54% (620/1,795).
2. **Source-Level Diagnostic Patterns:** Within the FruitVision B Fresh Apple subset ($N = 765$), exactly 174 images are correctly classified as Fresh Apple, while 345 are predicted as Rotten Apple, 242 as Rotten Orange, 2 as Fresh Orange, and 2 as Rotten Banana. 4. **Observational Russeting Hypothesis:** Visual inspection of the misclassified FruitVision fresh apple imagery reveals prominent natural surface striations, lenticel spots, and specular reflections characteristic of commercial varietal russeting. We hypothesize that the frozen MobileNetV2 backbone, trained predominantly on smooth, uniform laboratory specimens, correlates surface texture irregularities with fungal decay lesions. We explicitly present this russeting correlation as an observational diagnostic hypothesis rather than a causally verified physiological mechanism.

Note that because stratified bootstrap resampling was conducted independently per cohort evaluation ($B = 1,000$), resampled draws of the shared in-distribution reference subset (FruitVision B, $N_{\text{id}} = 4,157$) varied across runs, yielding identical point estimates ($41.98\%$ for Dual-Gate, $41.42\%$ for standalone Energy) alongside minor variations in empirical percentile bounds across Cohorts A, B, and C. Consequently, retaining automated sorting decisions on only 57.57% of known-class image records presents substantial operational abstention burden.
2. **Operational False Acceptance Rates:** On FruitVision Semantic Shift (Cohort A), standalone Energy restricts UFAR to 13.18% [12.07%, 14.34%] (366/2,776 accepted), while Dual-Gate allows 16.07% [14.77%, 17.40%] (446/2,776 accepted). In Cohort C (Combined Shift), UFAR is further suppressed to 6.86% (Energy) and 9.54% (Dual-Gate). However, in Cohort D (Sultana Semantic Shift), operational false acceptance rises steeply to 34.15% (Energy) and 34.90% [33.00%, 36.95%] (Dual-Gate), We observe weaker empirical discrimination and higher operational false acceptance on Cohort D (UFAR = 34.90%) than Cohort A (16.07%). While morphological or chromatic commonalities between certain unseen classes in Sultana D1 (such as green guava or jujube) and known green apples or citrus may represent an intuitive diagnostic hypothesis, visual similarity was not formally quantified via feature distance or perceptual metrics; we therefore present this class-overlap explanation strictly as an untested observational hypothesis.
3. **Dataset-Reported Treatment Routing Failure:** Under locked validation thresholds, 821 out of 1,917 treatment-labelled images (42.83% [41.26%, 44.34%]) in FruitVision C are accepted by Dual-Gate and assigned one of the classifier\'s Fresh-class output labels ($\hat{y} \in \{0, 1, 2\}$). Standalone Energy exhibits an even higher UFAR-Fresh (rate of acceptance and assignment to Fresh-class output labels) of 44.03% (844/1,917). Under the evaluated operating thresholds, a substantial fraction of treatment-labelled image records were accepted and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels. This outcome does not establish that their confidence-score distributions match those of untreated fresh fruit.
4. **Food-Safety Scope Disclaimer:** We emphasize that UFAR-Fresh measures strictly an image-level classifier routing outcome (acceptance and assignment of one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels); it does not represent verified biological food safety, commercial quality certification, or toxicological clearance.

## 5.5. Uncertainty Quantification and Consolidated Benchmark Findings

Synthesizing the empirical evidence from Sections 5.1 through 5.4, we consolidate the benchmark findings with respect to the study's three core research questions:

1. **Addressing RQ1 (External Domain Robustness and Spoilage Misclassification):** The frozen MobileNetV2 classifier suffers a marked 28.56 percentage-point degradation in top-1 accuracy (from 96.92% to 68.36%) when evaluated on external photographic repository images (Partition B). The primary failure mode is asymmetric collapse on Fresh Apples (32.12% accuracy; 310/965 correct), where misclassifications frequently fall into decay classes (393 predicted Rotten Apple, 255 Rotten Orange out of 965). Qualitative visual inspection indicates natural surface striations, lenticel spots, and specular reflections consistent with varietal russeting; we present this texture correlation strictly as an untested observational hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism.
2. **Addressing RQ2 (Comparative Efficacy under Species vs. Condition Shifts):** Under source-stratified semantic shift, Energy-based scoring (AUROC = 81.57% on FruitVision, 67.52% on Sultana) and Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis distance (81.41% and 68.15%) substantially outperform standard MSP (72.42% and 59.77%). However, under dataset-reported treatment-labelled condition shift (Cohort B), all evaluated scoring algorithms exhibit weak or near-chance discrimination (Energy AUROC = 53.13%, Mahalanobis = 47.91%, MSP = 58.77%). Tested RGB post-hoc scoring functions did not reliably separate images recorded under the dataset-reported treatment protocol from natural fruit.
3. **Addressing RQ3 (Operational Dual-Gate Feasibility and Trade-offs):** Validation-calibrated decision gating achieves partial unknown screening on semantic shifts (UFAR = 16.07% in A, 9.54% in C), but imposes a substantial operational abstention burden: 42.43% of known-class image records are rejected across external repository images (KRR = 41.98% on FruitVision, 44.01% on Sultana), though physical operational implications and sorting costs were not directly measured in this offline study. Crucially, 42.83% of dataset-reported treatment-labelled image records (821/1,917) are accepted by the Dual-Gate and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels, demonstrating that the evaluated visual OOD gates did not provide reliable screening on this treatment cohort.
4. **Methodological Conditioning and Statistical Non-Independence:** All reported confidence intervals are conditional on image-level resampling exchangeability ($B = 1,000$). Although Cohorts A--C share the same underlying FruitVision known-class reference records ($N_{\text{id}} = 4,157$), bootstrap resampling was performed independently for each cohort evaluation. Therefore, the marginal confidence intervals should not be interpreted as paired intervals for differences between cohorts. These cohorts are not independent experimental replications.
