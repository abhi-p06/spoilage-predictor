\section{Experimental Results and Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

This section presents the closed-set performance evaluation across partitions, source-stratified OOD detection AUROC and FPR@95 metrics, operational Dual-Gate rejection rates (UFAR, UFAR-Fresh, KRR), non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals, and sensitivity analysis across perceptual-hash (pHash) near-duplicate exclusion tiers.

\label{sec:results_sensitivity}

Table~\ref{tab:phash_sensitivity} reports the complete empirical sensitivity evaluation across all five post-hoc scoring functions and the exploratory Dual-Gate linear-margin proxy for all four source-matched cohorts. The sensitivity analysis demonstrates two critical empirical findings:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{High Metric Stability:} Across all 24 evaluated detector-cohort configurations, metric fluctuations between the Primary benchmark and Tier 2 remain minor: the maximum observed change in AUROC across all methods is $0.22$ percentage points (Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis in Cohort C, shifting from 80.08\% to 79.86\%). For most scoring functions, AUROC shifts are bounded within $\pm 0.11$ percentage points. In Cohort D (Sultana et al.), where zero cross-partition or multi-label pHash collisions were detected at $r \le 8$, metrics remain completely invariant.
\item \textbf{Strict Detector Ranking Invariance:} In all four cohorts, the relative ranking of detection methods is preserved without a single rank inversion across the three evaluation tiers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Cohort A (FruitVision Semantic Shift):} Energy (81.57\% $\to$ 81.46\%) $>$ Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis (81.41\% $\to$ 81.27\%) $>$ Dual-Gate Proxy (80.93\% $\to$ 80.82\%) $>$ Modified OpenMax (72.46\% $\to$ 72.38\%) $>$ MSP (72.42\% $\to$ 72.33\%) $>$ ODIN Variant (72.30\% $\to$ 72.20\%).
\item \textit{Cohort B (FruitVision Treatment Shift):} MSP (58.77\% $\to$ 58.81\%) $>$ ODIN Variant (56.73\% $\to$ 56.78\%) $>$ Modified OpenMax (55.35\% $\to$ 55.40\%) $>$ Dual-Gate Proxy (53.96\% $\to$ 54.02\%) $>$ Energy (53.13\% $\to$ 53.19\%) $>$ Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis (47.91\% $\to$ 48.01\%).
\item \textit{Cohort C (FruitVision Combined Shift):} Energy (84.72\% $\to$ 84.65\%) $>$ Dual-Gate Proxy (84.16\% $\to$ 84.10\%) $>$ Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis (80.08\% $\to$ 79.86\%) $>$ ODIN Variant (78.11\% $\to$ 78.13\%) $>$ Modified OpenMax (77.22\% $\to$ 77.22\%) $>$ MSP (76.47\% $\to$ 76.50\%).
\item \textit{Cohort D (Sultana Semantic Shift):} Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis (68.15\%) $>$ Energy (67.52\%) $>$ Dual-Gate Proxy (66.68\%) $>$ Modified OpenMax (64.38\%) $>$ ODIN Variant (60.02\%) $>$ MSP (59.77\%) (invariant across all three tiers).
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent \textit{Evidence Bounds on Sensitivity Conclusions:} These results establish that the benchmark performance estimates and comparative method orderings are robust with respect to the tested pHash collision exclusions and evaluated image records. However, we qualify this conclusion: perceptual hashing at low-frequency DCT resolution captures detectable visual near-duplicates, but cannot prove the complete absence of subtle burst captures, viewpoint variations, or unmodelled specimen-level correlations in the underlying repositories. To diagnose the drivers of this external degradation, Table~\ref{tab:classwise_performance_b} breaks down classification performance across individual classes and species. Inspection reveals that domain degradation is highly asymmetric:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Catastrophic Fresh Apple Collapse:} Fresh Apple accuracy collapses to 32.12\% (310/965 correct). The classifier systematically misidentifies 40.73\% of fresh apples as Rotten Apple (393 images) and 26.42\% as Rotten Orange (255 images). Across the entire Apple species cohort ($N = 1,795$), overall accuracy is only 34.54\% (620/1,795).
\item \textbf{Source-Level Diagnostic Patterns:} Within the FruitVision B Fresh Apple subset ($N = 765$), exactly 174 images are correctly classified as Fresh Apple, while 345 are predicted as Rotten Apple, 242 as Rotten Orange, 2 as Fresh Orange, and 2 as Rotten Banana. Within the Sultana B Fresh Orange subset ($N = 193$), 98 images are correct Fresh Orange, while 9 are predicted Fresh Apple, 15 Rotten Apple, and 71 Rotten Orange.
\item \textbf{High Banana and Orange Fidelity:} In contrast, Banana classification remains resilient at 87.80\% overall accuracy (1,562/1,779), with Rotten Banana achieving 99.40\% accuracy (826/831). Orange classification achieves 83.05\% overall accuracy (1,475/1,776), though Rotten Orange exhibits modest confusion with Fresh Orange (12.77\%).
\item \textbf{Observational Russeting Hypothesis:} Visual inspection of the misclassified FruitVision fresh apple imagery reveals prominent natural surface striations, lenticel spots, and specular reflections characteristic of commercial varietal russeting. We hypothesize that the frozen MobileNetV2 backbone, trained predominantly on smooth, uniform laboratory specimens, correlates surface texture irregularities with fungal decay lesions. We explicitly present this russeting correlation as an observational diagnostic hypothesis rather than a causally verified physiological mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical results across the four source-matched cohorts illuminate sharp performance disparities depending on the nature of the distribution shift:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Comparative Performance of Energy and Feature Scoring on Semantic Shifts:} In Cohort A (FruitVision Semantic Shift), Energy scoring achieves the highest discrimination (AUROC = 81.57\% [80.81\%, 82.38\%], FPR@95 = 68.84\%), closely followed by Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis (AUROC = 81.41\%, FPR@95 = 79.00\%) and the Energy--MSP linear-margin proxy (exploratory) (AUROC = 80.93\% [80.13\%, 81.72\%], FPR@95 = 69.27\%). Standard MSP (72.42\%), the ODIN-style variant (72.30\%), and modified OpenMax (72.46\%) lag by approximately 9 percentage points in AUROC, with retrospective false positive rates exceeding 82\%. In Cohort C (Combined Shift), the observed AUROC for Energy was higher (84.72\% [83.85\%, 85.58\%]) than in Cohort A (81.57\%) under the evaluated cohort definitions. However, because Cohort C evaluates a distinct subset of unseen species bearing dataset-reported treatment labels (Partition D2) rather than an isolated treatment intervention on Cohort A, this comparison reflects the empirical cohort ordering and cannot isolate a causal treatment effect.
\item \textbf{Weak Discrimination under Treatment-Labelled Condition Shift (Cohort B):} Under Cohort B, where identical known species (apple, banana, orange) are evaluated under dataset-reported treatment labels (formalin preservative immersion condition), all evaluated post-hoc scoring algorithms exhibit weak or near-chance discrimination. Energy AUROC is 53.13\% [52.16\%, 54.09\%], the Energy--MSP linear-margin proxy (exploratory) is 53.96\% [52.96\%, 54.91\%], modified OpenMax is 55.35\%, the ODIN variant is 56.73\%, and MSP is 58.77\%. Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis falls below chance (AUROC = 47.91\%, FPR@95 = 98.07\%). Retrospective false positive rates exceed 91\% across all methods, indicating that at a 95\% known retention operating point, virtually all images bearing dataset-reported treatment labels are accepted into the known class distribution.
\item \textbf{Domain-Dependent Morphological Confounding (Cohort D):} In Cohort D (Sultana Semantic Shift), discrimination is notably lower than in FruitVision Semantic Shift (Cohort A): Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis achieves 68.15\% AUROC, Energy achieves 67.52\% [65.67\%, 69.25\%], and MSP achieves only 59.77\%. Retrospective FPR@95 ranges from 81.20\% to 94.75\%. As an untested observational hypothesis, this lower discrimination may relate to visual commonalities among spherical green fruit classes (such as guava and jujube in Sultana D1 and green apples or citrus in the known set); however, this visual resemblance was not formally quantified or causally tested.
\item \textbf{Exploratory Energy--MSP Proxy Properties:} The Energy--MSP linear-margin proxy (exploratory) achieves AUROC values (80.93\% in A, 53.96\% in B, 84.16\% in C, 66.68\% in D) that mirror Energy scoring within 0.84 percentage points across all cohorts. As established in Section~\ref{sec:continuous_proxy}, this alignment occurs because unweighted linear combination of unbounded Energy with bounded MSP is mathematically dominated by Energy variance ($\sigma \approx 2.15$ vs. $\sigma \approx 0.18$), yielding a Spearman rank correlation $\rho > 0.992$ between the proxy and raw Energy scores across all 13,465 evaluated image records.
\end{enumerate}

Analysis of operational screening reveals notable operational trade-offs and rejection/acceptance outcomes:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Severe In-Distribution Abstention Penalty:} Operating thresholds calibrated to accept 95.15\% of primary in-distribution validation images suffer substantial over-rejection when transferred to external repository images. Standalone Energy exhibits an operational Known-Class Rejection Rate (KRR) of 41.42\% on FruitVision B and 41.74\% on Sultana B (41.50\% pooled). Dual-Gate exhibits a KRR of 41.98\% [40.75\%, 43.06\%] on FruitVision B and 44.01\% [41.41\%, 46.61\%] on Sultana B, pooling at 42.43\% [41.10\%, 43.76\%] (2,270/5,350 known images rejected). Note that because stratified bootstrap resampling was conducted independently per cohort evaluation ($B = 1,000$), resampled draws of the shared in-distribution reference subset (FruitVision B, $N_{\text{id}} = 4,157$) varied across runs, yielding identical point estimates ($41.98\%$ for Dual-Gate, $41.42\%$ for standalone Energy) alongside minor variations in empirical percentile bounds across Cohorts A, B, and C. Consequently, retaining automated sorting decisions on only 57.57\% of known-class image records presents substantial operational abstention overhead.
\item \textbf{Operational False Acceptance Rates:} On FruitVision Semantic Shift (Cohort A), standalone Energy restricts UFAR to 13.18\% [12.07\%, 14.34\%] (366/2,776 accepted), while Dual-Gate allows 16.07\% [14.77\%, 17.40\%] (446/2,776 accepted). In Cohort C (Combined Shift), UFAR is further suppressed to 6.86\% (Energy) and 9.54\% (Dual-Gate). However, in Cohort D (Sultana Semantic Shift), operational false acceptance rises steeply to 34.15\% (Energy) and 34.90\% [33.00\%, 36.95\%] (Dual-Gate), establishing weaker discrimination and higher operational false acceptance in Cohort D than in Cohort A. While visual commonalities among unseen species (such as guava and jujube in Sultana D1) and known classes represent an untested observational hypothesis, morphological geometry, color distributions, and feature-space overlap were not directly measured, and visual similarity cannot be inferred as the causal driver of this difference.
\item \textbf{Dataset-Reported Treatment Routing Failure:} Under locked validation thresholds, 821 out of 1,917 treatment-labelled image records (42.83\% [41.26\%, 44.34\%]) in FruitVision C are accepted by the Dual-Gate and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels ($\hat{y} \in \{0, 1, 2\}$). Standalone Energy exhibits an even higher UFAR-Fresh of 44.03\% (844/1,917). Under the evaluated operating thresholds, a substantial fraction of treatment-labelled image records were accepted and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels. This outcome does not establish that their confidence-score distributions match those of untreated fresh fruit.
\item \textbf{Food-Safety Scope Disclaimer:} We emphasize that UFAR-Fresh measures strictly the proportion of OOD image records accepted by the evaluated gate and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels; it is not claimed to represent verified biological freshness, commercial fruit-grade certification, microbial non-contamination, or toxicological clearance.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace*{-4pt}\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification and Consolidated Benchmark Findings}
\label{sec:results_consolidated_findings}

Synthesizing the empirical evidence from Sections~\ref{sec:results_sensitivity} through~\ref{sec:results_operational_rejection}, we consolidate the benchmark findings with respect to the study's three core research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Addressing RQ1 (External Domain Robustness and Spoilage Misclassification):} The frozen MobileNetV2 classifier suffers a marked 28.56 percentage-point degradation in top-1 accuracy (from 96.92\% to 68.36\%) when evaluated on external repository images. The primary failure mode is asymmetric collapse on Fresh Apples (32.12\% accuracy; 310/965 correct), where misclassifications frequently fall into decay classes (393 predicted Rotten Apple, 255 Rotten Orange out of 965). Qualitative visual inspection indicates natural surface striations, lenticel spots, and specular reflections consistent with varietal russeting; we present this texture correlation strictly as an untested observational hypothesis rather than a demonstrated causal mechanism.
\item \textbf{Addressing RQ2 (Comparative Efficacy under Species vs. Condition Shifts):} Under source-stratified semantic shift, Energy-based scoring (AUROC = 81.57\% on FruitVision, 67.52\% on Sultana) and Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis distance (81.41\% and 68.15\%) substantially outperform standard MSP (72.42\% and 59.77\%). However, under dataset-reported treatment-labelled condition shift (Cohort B), all evaluated scoring algorithms exhibit weak or near-chance discrimination (Energy AUROC = 53.13\%, Mahalanobis = 47.91\%, MSP = 58.77\%). Tested RGB post-hoc scoring functions did not reliably separate images recorded under the dataset-reported treatment protocol from natural fruit.
\item \textbf{Addressing RQ3 (Operational Dual-Gate Feasibility and Trade-offs):} Validation-calibrated decision gating achieves partial unknown screening on semantic shifts (UFAR = 16.07\% in A, 9.54\% in C), but imposes a substantial operational abstention burden: 42.43\% of known-class image records are rejected / abstained across external repository conditions (KRR = 41.98\% on FruitVision, 44.01\% on Sultana). Crucially, 42.83\% of dataset-reported treatment-labelled image records (821/1,917) are accepted by the Dual-Gate and assigned one of the classifier's Fresh-class output labels, demonstrating that the evaluated visual OOD gates did not provide reliable screening on this treatment cohort. Because physical conveyance, staffing overhead, and financial expenditure were not measured, evaluating real-world operational trade-offs requires dedicated field validation.
\item \textbf{Methodological Conditioning and Statistical Non-Independence:} All reported confidence intervals are conditional on image-level resampling exchangeability ($B = 1,000$). Although Cohorts A--C share the same underlying FruitVision known-class reference records ($N_{\text{id}} = 4,157$), bootstrap resampling was performed independently for each cohort evaluation. Therefore, the marginal confidence intervals should not be interpreted as paired intervals for differences between cohorts. These cohorts are not independent experimental replications.
\end{enumerate}
